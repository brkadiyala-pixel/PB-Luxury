Palm Beach County Luxury Market Report — Spring 2026

Spring 2026

Where the market stands

Palm Beach County's luxury segment held firm through the winter season, with the scarcest inventory concentrated at the very top of the market — waterfront estates on Palm Beach island and Manalapan continue to see multiple qualified offers within the first two to three weeks of listing.

Inland golf-community product, by contrast, has more breathing room. Buyers in the $3M–$6M range in communities like Old Palm and Frenchman's Creek have more selection and more negotiating leverage than they did twelve months ago, particularly on homes that have been on market longer than 60 days.

What's driving demand

Relocation activity from the Northeast and Midwest remains the single largest demand driver, with buyers citing tax treatment, year-round usability, and school quality as the top three reasons for choosing Palm Beach County over other Florida markets.

New construction continues to command a meaningful premium over resale — often 20-30% on a price-per-square-foot basis in comparable locations — which is pulling more move-up buyers toward pre-construction condo product in West Palm Beach and Boca Raton rather than competing for scarce waterfront resale inventory.

What sellers should know

Pricing precision matters more than it did two years ago. Overpriced listings are sitting longer and eventually trading below where a correctly priced listing would have closed — the market has shifted from "list high and negotiate" to "price accurately and let demand compete."

For sellers considering a move, off-market marketing before a public listing continues to be an effective way to test price with qualified buyers without accumulating days-on-market against the listing.
